# Lantern Pixel

**Philosophy:** a poster is a stage at the moment the curtain rises. One world, lit from within; a small cast stepping into its light. Nothing explains; the scene is the invitation.

**Space and form.** Every poster is split by light, not by lines. One side is a calm field of the event's own colour, where words can rest; the other side is depth: sky, far silhouettes, a near ledge, and the cast standing on it. The boundary between the two is a dithered dissolve built from an ordered 4×4 matrix, so the transition itself is made of pixels, placed one by one with painstaking care, never a smooth digital fade.

**Colour and material.** The world is painted in a narrow, deliberate palette: one dominant hue per event, its deep shadow, and a single warm accent that only the light sources may use (moon, lightning, window glow). Figures keep their native colours; atmosphere reaches them only through distance. Characters further back are tinted toward the sky, as if seen through dusk air, so depth reads instantly without a single extra mark. Every pixel is meant to look meticulously placed, the product of master-level patience.

**Scale and rhythm.** The cast is a chord, not a crowd: one lead in front at full size, companions half a step behind and a breath higher, alternating left and right so the silhouette rises like a mountain. Integer pixel scales only. Nothing is resampled, nothing is blurred; the grid is sacred and is kept crisp through every export.

**Hierarchy.** Light first, the lead figure second, the field of colour third. Text is not painted into the art at all; the poster leaves a quiet zone and lets the interface set the words. Small ground shadows anchor each figure to the ledge, so the cast feels present rather than pasted. The finished piece should look labored over for days by someone at the very top of the craft: cohesive, restrained, and impossible to improve by adding anything more.
